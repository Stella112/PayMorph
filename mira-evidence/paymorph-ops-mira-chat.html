Goal: Preserve capital, earn modest yield on USDT.

• Hold USDT in wallet — no smart-contract risk, fully liquid for withdrawals or refunds.
• STON.fi Liquidity Pool: USDT/TON — provide USDT + TON as liquidity. Earns trading fees from swaps. Impermanent loss is real but manageable in a stable/volatile pair; the USDT side stays relatively stable.
• What to watch: Pool APY can fluctuate. Withdraw liquidity before it drops below your target threshold.

Expected yield: 2–8% APY (trading fees, no token rewards)



🟡 2. Moderate-Risk Strategy — "Earn & Grow"
Goal: Boost yield using STON.fi incentives while staying TON-native.

• STON.fi Liquidity Pool: USDT/TON — same as above, but also stake your LP tokens in STON.fi's farming contract if available for that pair.
• STON token rewards from farming can be held (exposure to STON price) or swapped back to USDT immediately via Omniston.
• Optional: Swap a portion of accumulated USDT into stTON (liquid staked TON) for ~4% staking yield, keeping the rest in USDT for liquidity.

What to watch: STON reward emissions can taper off. Impermanent loss widens if TON moves sharply. stTON peg can wobble briefly.

Expected yield: 5–15% APY (fees + farming rewards)

**Withdraw Liquidity**  | Pull funds out when you need USDT for business expenses

🔄 What STON.fi Omniston Does
Omniston is the swap engine that makes "pay TON, receive USDT" work. Here's what it actually does:



1. Quotes the price
When the customer opens the payment link, Omniston checks all available liquidity pools on TON and calculates: "How much TON does the customer need to send right now to produce exactly 0.01 USDT?"

This is a fixed-output quote — the merchant always gets the exact amount they asked for. The TON side adjusts.

2. Finds the best route
There might be multiple paths to get from TON to USDT:

• TON → USDT (direct pool)
• TON → stTON → USDT (two hops)
• TON → some other token → USDT

Omniston compares routes and picks the one with the best rate and lowest fees for the customer.

3. Handles slippage
Crypto prices move every second. Omniston builds in a slippage tolerance (recommended 0.5%) so minor price shifts between quote and execution don't cause the swap to fail.

4. Executes the swap on-chain
Once the customer approves the transaction in TonConnect, Omniston's route executes as a single atomic transaction on the blockchain. Either the full swap completes, or nothing moves. No partial payments, no stuck funds.



What Omniston does NOT do
• ❌ It does not hold customer funds
• ❌ It does not sign transactions — the customer does
• ❌ It does not decide the invoice amount — the merchant does
• ❌ It does not mark invoices paid — PayMorph does that only after on-chain confirmation



When is Omniston NOT needed?
TON invoices. If the merchant chooses to receive TON, it's a direct wallet-to-wallet transfer. No swap, no Omniston, no slippage. Just TON in, TON out.



TL;DR: Omniston is the matchmaker — it finds the cheapest way to turn the customer's TON into the merchant's USDT/USDC, quotes the price, and executes the swap in one on-chain transaction. The customer approves, Omniston delivers. 🔀
